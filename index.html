    問The problem
    
Code is written in English. Most of the people writing it aren’t native speakers.

    
More than 180 million developers build on GitHub, and roughly 5 in 6 of them are outside the United States.1 They are fluent in code, but every doc, error message, issue thread and meeting adds a small language tax. It never shows up in a sprint report, yet it’s paid every day.

  

  

    
    
      
  1. 09:40
    A new library. The README is in English, the best issue thread is in Japanese. Two browser tabs and a translation site later, back to the editor.
    2. 
      
  2. 11:15
    A red stack trace. The words are clear one by one, but what it actually means takes ten minutes of searching.
    3. 
      
  3. 14:00
    Writing a PR description. Drafted in Chinese, pasted into a translator, fixed by hand so it doesn’t sound machine-made.
    4. 
      
  4. 16:30
    Stand-up with the overseas team. Not sure whether it’s “ku-ber-NEET-ees” or “koo-ber-NET-eez”, so the word gets avoided.
    5. 
    

    

      
The tools exist, but they live in other windows, they don’t understand code, and they forget you every time.

      
Translation sites mangle code and terminology. AI chat assistants answer when asked, in English first, and start from zero in every conversation. Nothing sits where the reading actually happens, speaks the words out loud, and remembers who you are.

    

  






  

    能What TA does
    
Wherever you get stuck, he’s already there.

    
TA isn’t another window to switch to. He sits right where you’re reading, and speaks up when you pause.

  

  

    
He helps you read
Hover over an English comment, a README or a Japanese issue and TA translates it into your language. Terms like map() and Promise stay untouched, and code is never translated.

    
He reads it aloud
Any text, read in English, Japanese, Korean, Spanish and more. Listen when your eyes are tired, or rehearse with him before a meeting.

    
He helps you debug
When the terminal turns red, TA tells you in plain words what happened, which line is most likely the cause, and what to try next.

    
He helps you speak up
Draft commit messages, PR descriptions and issue replies in your own language. TA rewrites them in natural engineering English and lets you compare before you send.

    
He reads code with you
Taking over someone else’s project? Select a block and ask “what does this do?” TA explains it step by step, in your language.

    
He remembers you
Your languages, the words you keep looking up, how much detail you like. TA remembers, so you never start from zero.

A good friend doesn’t need the backstory every time.

    
Most translation tools treat you like a stranger on every request. TA remembers your habits and your projects, so his explanations land at the right level. Memories stay in your account, and you can view or delete them anytime.

    時Why now
    
Three things changed in the last two years.

  

  

    
譯
Translation that understands code
Frontier models like Claude now translate technical text accurately, keep identifiers intact, and explain an error instead of just converting its words.

    
聲
Natural voices in every language
Speech synthesis now sounds human in dozens of languages, and is cheap enough to read every doc and comment aloud on demand.

    
記
Assistants that can remember
Long context and memory let an assistant keep a lasting picture of one developer: their languages, projects and habits. That turns a tool into a companion.

    答Questions
    
Frequently asked.

  

  

    
Which languages does TA support?
At launch: Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Spanish, translating from and into English. Read-aloud works in any language your system or our cloud voices support.

    
Is my code sent anywhere?
Only the text you hover or select is sent for translation. TA never uploads whole files on its own, never trains models on your code, and you can turn memory off at any time.

    
Which editors are supported?
VS Code first, including editors built on it. A web app for docs and articles comes next. Other editors depend on what our first users ask for.

    
How is this different from GitHub Copilot?
Copilot helps you write code. TA helps you understand and communicate around it: reading, listening, explaining and speaking. They work well side by side.

    
When can I try it?
We are opening a private test for a small group first. Join the waitlist and tell us how you work. We reply to every email personally.
